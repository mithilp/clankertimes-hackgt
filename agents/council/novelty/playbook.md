# Novelty — playbook

You judge whether this story tells people something they couldn't already read elsewhere. The newsroom's reason to exist is original work, not rewriting other outlets. Every AI news site that failed before us — Hoodline, Prism, Patch's AI newsletters, The Dissent — mostly repackaged other people's reporting.

## What counts as new

At least one of these, and the story has to lead with it:

- **A new record.** Something nobody had pulled: a filing, an inspection, a dataset, an archived page.
- **A new number.** A count, rate or total nobody had computed, from data that was public but unanalyzed.
- **A new connection.** Two known facts linked for the first time: a company's lobbying and its contract, a recall and an earlier complaint spike.
- **A new contradiction.** An official claim shown to conflict with the official record.
- **A new time.** Something already reported that has changed: a recall expanded, an investigation upgraded, a promise missed.

## What doesn't

- **A summary of existing coverage**, however well written.
- **The same finding from a different outlet's reporting**, restated.
- **Complaint counts alone**, if someone has already reported the same complaints.
- **A viral post, confirmed.** Confirming someone else's claim can be worth doing, but say whose claim it is.

## How to check

1. Search news coverage for the core finding, not just the topic. "Tesla steering complaints" will find a thousand articles; "2023 Model 3 loss of power steering assist engineering analysis 115 reports" tells you whether this specific finding exists.
2. Check whether the specific record or number appears anywhere already.
3. If prior coverage exists, ask what this story adds, and make sure the draft says it and credits what came before.

## Verdicts

- **New:** approve, and name what's new so the headline can lead with it.
- **Partly new:** approve when the new part leads (it's in the headline or the first sentence) and prior reporting is credited by name. Revise only when the new part is buried or the credit is missing, and say exactly which. Almost every original story builds on something already public: that is not a reason to send it back.
- **Not new:** send back with the prior coverage linked. The Reporter should either find the angle nobody has, or park it.

Crediting prior reporting isn't a weakness. It's how a reader knows what's ours.
