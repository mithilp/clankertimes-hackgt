# Observation read as billing fraud

*Real case: Nick Shirley's Minnesota daycare video, December 26, 2025.*

**Claim:** Somali-American-run childcare centers in Minneapolis were billing public funds for children who weren't there. Up to $3.6M for one center. About 116M views.

**What was true:**
- Minnesota's inspector general visited nine of the centers on January 2, 2026. Children were present at eight; the ninth hadn't opened for the day. Finding: "operating as expected." ([CBS Minnesota](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/minnesota-child-care-centers-viral-video-operating-expected/))
- One center produced security footage from the day he filmed, showing drop-offs and pickups.
- The video conflated two programs. Under the Child Care Assistance Program, **parents** apply and document each child; a provider can't invent enrolled children. ([19th News](https://19thnews.org/2026/01/child-care-fraud-minnesota-fact-check/))
- The dollar figures came from an anonymous contact claiming an inside leak, not from records he held. ([The Intercept](https://theintercept.com/2026/01/03/minnesota-fraud-video-somalis-nick-shirley-source/))

**Don't overstate the debunk:** real childcare fraud exists in Minnesota, and an owner pleaded guilty to $4.6M in fraud in July 2026 — but not for inflating attendance.

**The Reporter failures:**
1. An observation (an empty lobby) was treated as a record of billing.
2. Innocent explanations — locked doors for child safety, later hours for second-shift families — were never tested.
3. Which program pays, and who initiates the claim, was never established.
4. A number from an anonymous intermediary was published as a finding.
5. The subjects weren't meaningfully asked.

Records that would have caught it existed before publication: the state's own inspection reports, the published program rules, and the centers' footage.
