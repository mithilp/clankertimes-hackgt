# Beat: Atlanta

## Focus

Everything about how metro Atlanta is run, and who pays for it. The City of Atlanta (the mayor's office, City Council, and city departments), the metro counties (Fulton, DeKalb, Cobb, Gwinnett, Clayton), the authorities and agencies that spend public money here (MARTA, Hartsfield-Jackson, Invest Atlanta, the Atlanta Housing Authority, Atlanta BeltLine Inc., Atlanta Public Schools and the county school districts), the state government under the Gold Dome as it lands on Atlanta, and the large companies and developers whose deals run through those bodies.

This is a wide beat. Gather broadly every pass. Watch everything and report little: most of what you see will be skipped, and that is correct.

## What "interesting" means on this beat

The playbook's four tests still apply: moving now, someone accountable, a checkable claim underneath, a records trail nobody has pulled. On this beat they tend to look like:

- **Public money and contracts.** Airport concessions and construction, city and county procurement, no-bid or emergency contracts, cost overruns, tax abatements from Invest Atlanta and county development authorities, tax allocation districts, stadium and arena deals, big-event spending and its audits.
- **Infrastructure that fails.** Water main breaks and boil-water notices, the sewer consent decree, MARTA outages and delayed projects, road and bridge work, 911 and emergency-service outages, power outages and Georgia Power rate cases before the Public Service Commission.
- **Housing and land.** Evictions, code enforcement, the housing authority's properties, rezonings and annexations, affordable-housing promises attached to developments, data centers and their tax breaks, water use and power demand.
- **Policing, courts and jails.** Use-of-force cases, the Fulton County jail, prosecutors' charging decisions, court backlogs, settlements the city or counties paid out.
- **Schools.** Budget votes, closures and consolidations, test-score releases, superintendent contracts, and school-district audits.
- **Elections and ethics.** Campaign finance, ethics-board complaints, inspector general and city auditor reports, officials' financial disclosures, and election administration in the metro counties.
- **Environment and health.** Air and water permits, EPA and state enforcement, landfill and industrial sites, heat, hospital closures and openings.

A pattern of posts on r/Atlanta is a lead when many unrelated people describe the same failure (a water outage, a MARTA line down, the same towing company). It is never the story by itself.

## What to skip

- Crime of the day: shootings, arrests and car crashes that no institution is accountable for. A pattern, a policy or a failure to respond can be a signal; one incident is not.
- Sports results, restaurant and events coverage, weather forecasts, traffic reports.
- Celebrity and entertainment news, unless public money or a public official is involved.
- Anything whose subject is a private person: a victim, a suspect not charged by officials, a neighbor in a dispute. Private people can appear in a story; they are never what it is about.
- Press releases announcing a ribbon-cutting or partnership with no dollar figure, contractor or vote attached.

## Where the records usually are

- Atlanta City Council agendas, legislation and votes; county commission agendas and minutes
- The City of Atlanta's Office of Inspector General, the City Auditor, and the Ethics Office
- Atlanta's and the counties' procurement portals and contract awards
- Invest Atlanta and county development-authority board minutes (tax abatements)
- Georgia Public Service Commission dockets (Georgia Power rates, large-load customers like data centers)
- Georgia Government Transparency and Campaign Finance Commission filings
- Georgia Open Records Act requests
- Fulton County Superior Court and federal court (Northern District of Georgia) dockets
- EPA ECHO enforcement records and Georgia EPD permits
- MARTA, Atlanta Housing and school-board meeting minutes and budgets
- Property records and deeds for the metro counties

## Searching this beat

- Keep news searches short and specific: an institution plus a topic ("Fulton County jail", "MARTA budget", "Invest Atlanta abatement"). Stacked OR lists return little.
- Keep sports out of broad searches: add `-football -basketball -Falcons -Hawks -Braves -United -odds`.
- "Georgia" alone collides with the country of Georgia. Always say "Atlanta", a county name, or "Georgia" plus a state institution.
- Web search is undated. Use it to find a record or a page, not to find what is new.
