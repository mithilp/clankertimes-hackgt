# Beat: Tech

## Focus

The technology industry as a set of powerful institutions: big platforms, AI labs, chipmakers, cloud and data-center operators, crypto firms, autonomous-vehicle companies and the startups that sell to governments. And the regulators, courts and legislatures that are supposed to check them. We watch what these companies do to users, workers, markets and communities, and what they tell regulators and investors about it.

Product launches and funding rounds are not the beat. What a company is accountable for is.

## What "interesting" means on this beat

The playbook's four tests still apply: moving now, someone accountable, a checkable claim underneath, a records trail nobody has pulled. On this beat they tend to look like:

- **Enforcement and lawsuits.** FTC, DOJ, SEC, CFPB and state attorney-general actions; private class actions; copyright suits against AI companies; EU and UK regulatory decisions; settlements and consent decrees and whether they're being kept.
- **Safety.** Autonomous-vehicle crashes and NHTSA investigations, product recalls, AI systems giving dangerous output, child-safety failures on platforms.
- **Security and outages.** Breaches (companies must now disclose material ones to the SEC), ransomware at hospitals and cities, cloud outages that took down public services.
- **Data centers, power and water.** Tax abatements, utility rate cases for large-load customers, water permits, local opposition, who pays for new generation and transmission.
- **Chips and export controls.** Commerce Department entity-list additions and license decisions, smuggling cases, CHIPS Act awards and whether promised factories are being built.
- **Labor.** Layoffs (WARN Act notices), union drives, gig-worker classification rulings, content-moderator conditions, H-1B use.
- **Government contracts.** Big awards to tech firms (defense, immigration, surveillance, AI), and what the contracts say.
- **Money.** Crypto collapses and frauds, accounting restatements, insider sales before bad news, what a company told investors versus what it told the public.

A burst of posts on Hacker News or r/technology is a lead when many people report the same failure (an outage, a policy change, a data leak). It is never the story by itself.

## What to skip

- Product launches, reviews, benchmarks, rumors about unreleased products, and "company X announces Y" with nothing at stake.
- Funding rounds and valuations, unless the money is public or the round hides a problem.
- Personal drama about founders and executives that no record could settle.
- Opinion essays, hype threads, and predictions.
- Individual users' complaints about a product with no pattern and no institutional decision behind them.

## Where the records usually are

- SEC EDGAR: 8-K filings (including cybersecurity incidents), 10-K risk factors, insider-sale Form 4s
- Federal and state court dockets (CourtListener/PACER); FTC and DOJ complaints and consent orders
- The Federal Register: FTC and FCC rules, Commerce Department (BIS) entity-list changes
- NHTSA investigations and recalls for vehicles with driver assistance or self-driving
- State WARN Act layoff notices
- USAspending.gov and agency contract announcements
- State public-utility commission dockets and county development-authority minutes for data centers
- EU Commission decisions under the Digital Markets Act and Digital Services Act

## Searching this beat

- Name the company and the institution: "Waymo NHTSA", "Nvidia export license", "Meta FTC". Company names alone return product news.
- Leave out "AI" by itself; it returns hype. Pair it with a regulator, a court or a harm.
- Web search is undated. Use it to find a record, not to find what is new.

## Where to look

Starting points, not limits:

- Hacker News and r/technology, for what engineers and users are noticing
- Feeds: Techmeme https://www.techmeme.com/feed.xml, 404 Media https://www.404media.co/rss/, Ars Technica https://feeds.arstechnica.com/arstechnica/index, The Verge https://www.theverge.com/rss/index.xml, TechCrunch https://techcrunch.com/feed/, Wired https://www.wired.com/feed/rss
- The Federal Register, searching for a company or a program by name
- Polymarket markets on tech companies and AI, for where the crowd thinks something changed
