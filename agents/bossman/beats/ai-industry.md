# Beat: The AI industry

## Focus

The companies building and selling AI, and the people and institutions affected by them: AI labs, the big platforms deploying models, chip and data-center suppliers, and the governments buying and regulating AI. An AI newsroom covering AI has to be the hardest grader in the room: hold these companies to what they claim, in records, not in press releases.

## What "interesting" means on this beat

The playbook's four tests still apply. On this beat they tend to look like:

- **Claims versus records.** A company's safety, accuracy or privacy claims contradicted by a court filing, a regulator's finding, a model card, or its own published evaluations.
- **Harms with a paper trail.** Lawsuits over AI outputs (defamation, product liability, deaths), deepfake cases, discrimination findings in hiring or lending tools.
- **Government AI contracts.** Awards to AI companies for defense, immigration, policing or benefits decisions, and what the contracts require.
- **Data centers and power.** Where the compute is being built, the tax breaks and utility deals behind it, and who pays.
- **Copyright and data.** Rulings and settlements over training data, licensing deals, and what was scraped.
- **Labor.** Layoffs attributed to AI, contractor and data-labeling conditions, and what companies told investors about it.
- **Regulation.** State AI laws taking effect, federal agency actions, EU AI Act enforcement, and whether companies comply.

## What to skip

- Model launches, benchmark scores, funding rounds and valuations with nothing at stake.
- Predictions, hype and doom threads, and opinion essays.
- Researchers' and executives' personal drama that no record could settle.
- Product demos and "company X adds AI to Y."

## Where the records usually are

- Federal and state court dockets (CourtListener/PACER): the lawsuits against AI companies
- SEC filings: risk factors, material events, what companies tell investors about AI
- USAspending.gov and agency contract announcements
- FTC complaints and orders; state attorney-general actions
- Model cards, system cards and published evaluations (companies' own documents)
- Utility-commission dockets and development-authority minutes for data centers
- The Federal Register for agency rules on AI

## Searching this beat

- Name the company and the institution: "OpenAI lawsuit", "Anthropic court", "AI contract Pentagon", "data center tax abatement".
- "AI" alone returns hype; always pair it with a court, a regulator, a contract or a harm.
