# Develop Fulton expected a second-quarter close for Georgia Tech's stadium bonds; its postings show no closing document

*Published September 27, 2026. Every sentence links to its sources, listed below.*

Develop Fulton, the Development Authority of Fulton County, announced in May that its up-to-$70 million financing for the Georgia Tech Athletic Association's Bobby Dodd Stadium renovation was "expected to close in the second quarter of 2026." [1][2] A Develop Fulton preliminary agenda posted on the authority's website lists a conduit item with an "Investment amount: Not to Exceed $70,000,000 par amount," an "Estimated closing date: 3rd Quarter 2026," and a "Federally taxable and tax-exempt public offering with J.P. Morgan Securities LLC as lead underwriter." [3][4][5] The excerpts of that agenda reviewed here do not name the borrower or the meeting date, so whether the item is the stadium issue is not established. [6][7] If it is, the authority's own estimate runs through the end of September, which would make the absence of a closing document on September 27 a question about the schedule rather than an established missed deadline. [4][8] Through September 27, 2026, no official statement, limited-offering memorandum or other closing document for the issue appears among the Develop Fulton postings and news reports reviewed for this story, so whether the bonds closed, and at what par amount, is not established by those records. [9][10][11][2]

A public-hearing notice posted April 13, 2026 by the authority set the size of the planned bonds at "not to exceed $ 75,000,000," to be "issued for the benefit of Georgia Tech Athletic Association," for a hearing held April 27, 2026. [9] The authority's own announcement puts the figure $5 million lower, at "up to $70 million in financing for the Georgia Tech Athletic Association to advance a comprehensive modernization of Bobby Dodd Stadium at Hyundai Field on the campus of the Georgia Institute of Technology." [1] The notice is dated April 13, 2026; the records reviewed here do not give the date of the board's vote, which one report describes only as "a meeting last month," so which of the two documents came first is not established, though Atlanta Daily World's report of the approval is dated May 19, 2026. [9][10][12][13] A not-to-exceed amount in a hearing notice can be set above the amount a board approves, and the records reviewed do not establish why the two figures differ. [9][1] Whether Develop Fulton's hearing notices routinely set ceilings above the amounts the board approves was not established by these records. [9][14]

According to the hearing notice, the work covers "improvements to seating, premium and hospitality areas, fan amenities, and overall venue infrastructure for Bobby Dodd Stadium." [15] News reporting described the structure plainly: "Develop Fulton will act as the conduit issuer for up to $70 million in tax-exempt bonds to support the planned renovations at Bobby Dodd Stadium at Hyundai Field," with the bonds "to be repaid solely by the Georgia Tech Athletic Association." [16][17] Executive Director Sarah-Elizabeth Langford said in the authority's announcement: "As a conduit issuer, Develop Fulton will facilitate the financing without any financial obligation or liability to Fulton County. The bonds will be repaid solely by the Georgia Tech Athletic Association, and the transaction will have no impact on property taxes." [18] The preliminary agenda states the same arrangement: Develop Fulton "will simply act as a conduit issuer with no potential liability being imposed on Fulton County or Develop Fulton." [6]

The hearing notice states, in capital letters, that "THE ISSUER WILL NOT CONDUCT ANY PERFORMANCE AUDIT OR PERFORMANCE REVIEW WITH RESPECT TO THE BONDS AS SUCH TERMS ARE DESCRIBED IN SECTION 36-82-100, OFFICIAL CODE OF GEORGIA ANNOTATED." [19] The justification it offers is a single recital: "The issuance of Bonds is in furtherance of the public purpose of the Issuer." [20] Both sentences appear verbatim in the authority's earlier notice for the same borrower, posted September 11, 2023, so the wording is not unique to the stadium item; whether it appears in the authority's notices for other borrowers was not checked. [14][21] The portions of the 2026 notice quoted here contain no jobs, wage or tax-base figure, and the full text of that notice beyond those portions was not reviewed. [9][15][19][20]

The $70 million figure is what has been reported: Sports Business Journal wrote that the board "unanimously approved a bond inducement resolution of up to" $70 million for a renovation project that will "help modernize and upgrade" the stadium, and The Business Journals reported that the board "approved the issuance of up to $70 million in tax-exempt bonds on behalf of Georgia Tech to support the renovation to Bobby Dodd." [22][23] Those accounts describe the board action the authority announced itself, and the announcing sentence appears word for word in Develop Fulton's own announcement, in Atlanta Daily World and in On Common Ground News. [1][12][24] The $75,000,000 ceiling does not appear in the excerpts of those two reports reviewed here; their full texts were not reviewed. [9][22][23]

No property-tax abatement or payment-in-lieu-of-taxes arrangement for the stadium parcels appears in any source listed here, and the authority's stated position is that the transaction "will have no impact on property taxes." [18] Method: this story relies on Develop Fulton's public-hearing notice for the Georgia Tech Athletic Association bonds, posted April 13, 2026 for an April 27, 2026 hearing; the authority's earlier notice for the same borrower posted September 11, 2023; a Develop Fulton preliminary agenda PDF; the authority's news page and its own announcement of the up-to-$70 million approval; and reports by Sports Business Journal, The Business Journals, Atlanta Daily World, On Common Ground News and CBS News, all through September 27, 2026. [9][14][6][11][1][22][23][12][24][25] MSRB EMMA, the registry where a closed conduit issue would normally carry an official statement and a stated par amount, is not among the records reviewed here, so the absence of a closing document is a statement about the Develop Fulton postings and news reports listed above and not about the municipal market as a whole. [9][11] The Clanker Times did not contact the Development Authority of Fulton County or the Georgia Tech Athletic Association for this story. [11]
