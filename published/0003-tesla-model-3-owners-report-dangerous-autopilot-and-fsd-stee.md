# Tesla Model 3 owners report dangerous Autopilot and FSD steering failures; recalls and federal probes follow

*Published September 26, 2026. Every sentence links to its sources, listed below.*

Fifteen owners of the 2023 Tesla Model 3 have reported to the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration that the car's Autopilot or Full Self-Driving systems failed to steer or control the vehicle properly, according to NHTSA's complaint database. [1]

One owner wrote that the "full self driving is very dangerous" and described events including "going to the wrong lane" and "changing lanes that almost hit a car." [1]

Another reported that the car "ran a red light on two separate occasions while in full self driving mode," and a third said the vehicle "suddenly turned towards the left onto railroad tracks" in rainy weather, damaging the tires and rims. [1]

Tesla has issued multiple recalls affecting the 2023 Model 3 that address steering and control issues. [2][3][4]

In February 2023, Tesla recalled vehicles equipped with Full Self-Driving Beta software because the system "may allow the vehicle to act unsafe around intersections, such as traveling straight through an intersection while in a turn-only lane, entering a stop sign-controlled intersection without coming to a complete stop, or proceeding into an intersection during a steady yellow traffic signal without due caution." [5]

Tesla said it would release an over-the-air software update free of charge to fix the issue. [6]

In December 2023, Tesla recalled vehicles with Autosteer because "the prominence and scope of the feature's controls may not be sufficient to prevent driver misuse of the SAE Level 2 advanced driver-assistance feature." [7]

The recall report noted that if a driver misuses Autosteer, "there may be an increased risk of a crash." [8]

In February 2025, Tesla recalled certain 2023 Model 3 vehicles for a loss of power steering assist. [4]

According to the recall, "the printed circuit board for the electronic power steering assist may experience an overstress condition, causing a loss of power steering assist when the vehicle reaches a stop and then accelerates again." [9]

Tesla stated that "a loss of power steering assist can require greater steering effort, especially at low speeds, increasing the risk of a crash." [10]

Tesla said it was "not aware of any crashes, injuries or deaths related to the condition." [11]

NHTSA opened a preliminary evaluation in July 2023 into 2023 Model 3 and Model Y vehicles "due to reports of inability to steer the vehicle and the loss of power steering." [12]

At that time, the agency had received twelve complaints alleging loss of steering control and power steering. [13]

The investigation was upgraded to an engineering analysis in May 2024, by which time NHTSA had received 115 reports of loss of steering control. [14][15]

The engineering analysis covers "loss of steering control, frequently accompanied by driver-facing messages indicating that power steering assist is reduced or disabled." [16]

Tesla has not publicly commented on the specific complaints in NHTSA's database, but the company has issued recalls and software updates to address the issues. [6][17]
